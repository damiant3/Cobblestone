 Reassembly against fragments THIS PROJECT DID NOT PRODUCE. Every other
 fragmentation test in the tree cuts a message with dtls-msg-fragments and
 rejoins it with DtlsReasm, which cannot tell a correct implementation from
 two consistently wrong ones -- the failure BrotliBeatsOpus.md exists to
 describe. These records are bytes off the wire from OpenSSL 3.2.4
 s_server, captured through a recording UDP proxy; the regeneration recipe
 is build/dtls-fragment-interop.ps1 -Regenerate.

 DTLS 1.2 is the version captured, and it is the RIGHT oracle for this
 chapter even though our endpoint is 1.3-only. The handshake message header
 and its fragmentation rules are byte-identical between RFC 6347 section
 4.2.2 and RFC 9147 section 5.2: same 12-byte header, same uint24
 fragment_offset and fragment_length, same requirement that the length
 field carry the WHOLE message. What differs between the two versions is
 the record layer and the key schedule, and neither is under test here. No
 DTLS 1.3 peer was available to capture: OpenSSL gained DTLS 1.3 in 3.5 and
 3.2.4 is what is installed.

 The capture handed us something better than a fragmented message. OpenSSL
 retransmitted the flight and REFRAGMENTED IT AT DIFFERENT BOUNDARIES:
 %s, then %s. RFC 9147 section 5.2 requires a receiver
 to tolerate exactly that, and until now the claim rested on our own
 generator, which cuts the same way twice and can never produce the case.

Section: Reassembly

  add-rec : DtlsReasm, List Integer -> DtlsReasm
  add-rec (r) (rec) =
    when dtls-plain-decode rec
      is None -> r
      is Just (p) -> add-frag r (p.dpr-fragment)

  add-frag : DtlsReasm, List Integer -> DtlsReasm
  add-frag (r) (bs) =
    when dtls-msg-parse bs
      is None -> r
      is Just (m) -> dtls-reasm-add r m

  digest-of : DtlsReasm -> Text
  digest-of (r) =
    when dtls-reasm-message r
      is None -> "incomplete"
      is Just (m) -> sha256-to-hex (sha256 (m.dm-body))

  shape-of : DtlsReasm -> Text
  shape-of (r) =
    when dtls-reasm-message r
      is None -> "none"
      is Just (m) -> "type=" & show (m.dm-type) & " len=" & show (m.dm-length) & " body=" & show (list-length (m.dm-body))

Section: The Arms

 One: the flight as OpenSSL first sent it.

  first : DtlsReasm = add-rec (add-rec (add-rec dtls-reasm-new ossl-x1-r0) ossl-x1-r1) ossl-x1-r2

 Two: the retransmission, cut at different offsets. Same bytes or the
 reassembler is sensitive to where the peer chose to cut, which is the
 defect this arm exists to catch.

  second : DtlsReasm = add-rec (add-rec (add-rec dtls-reasm-new ossl-x2-r0) ossl-x2-r1) ossl-x2-r2

 Three: the case a single implementation cannot generate. Fragments from
 BOTH cuts, out of order and overlapping, which is what a real receiver
 sees when a flight is retransmitted before the first copy finished
 arriving.

  mixed : DtlsReasm =
    add-rec (add-rec (add-rec (add-rec dtls-reasm-new ossl-x1-r0) ossl-x2-r1) ossl-x2-r0) ossl-x1-r2

 Four: the negative. Drop the middle fragment and the message must report
 incomplete rather than assemble short. A reassembler that answers with
 whatever it has is the one that hands a truncated Certificate to a parser.

  gapped : DtlsReasm = add-rec (add-rec dtls-reasm-new ossl-x1-r0) ossl-x1-r2
