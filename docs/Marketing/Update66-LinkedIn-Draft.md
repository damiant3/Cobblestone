Get your kicks on Update 66. 🛣️🇺🇸

Cobblestone just shipped its 66th public update, and it's the first "Update" post since 38. Twenty-eight releases of road behind us, so here are the highlights from the trip.

Cobblestone is a language, compiler and operating system we're building from first principles. The idea is simple: freedom is the goal. Own the whole road, from the bytes the compiler writes to the pixels on the screen, and verify every mile of it.

🗓️ **Real scheduling.** Every core now runs a preemptive 10 ms time slice. The first system service, the web server, moved into its own process. Each process keeps its own vector registers, and a 12,000,000-pass stress test went from 1 to 4 wrong answers per run to zero. On the desktop, a new scheduler gives each pane a turn instead of a loop of its own, and 3D View and the Aquarium now render in persistent worker processes. On real hardware, the whole-screen flashing is gone.

🧠 **The compiler cleaned out its decks.** The compiler manages memory in "decks": regions it reserves, fills and resets as it works. We found a case where it ran 9.3 MB past its reservation and quietly wrote wrong code. Now it refuses instead. A deck that overflows now faults loudly rather than corrupting the output. Reclaiming memory per definition halved the self-compile peak, from 1,147 MB to 537 MB. The test battery went from about 10.5 minutes to 123 seconds. And the compiler still rebuilds itself byte for byte, on bare metal and in your browser (2.46 million characters in 19 seconds).

🎨 **Diffusion, written from scratch.** Stable Diffusion XL now runs as GPU kernels our own compiler emits, graded against Forge, with all 25 samplers and 16 schedules. A 1024-pixel image went from 354 seconds to about 29. Flux runs too, along with the ESRGAN, SwinIR and DAT upscalers.

🌐 **And in a browser tab.** SD1.5 and SDXL run on WebGPU, within a fraction of a color step of the native engine, with LoRA in six formats. MusicGen turns a real checkpoint into audio, matched stage by stage against the reference. A local Qwen3 language model decodes token for token what llama.cpp produces. Spark Studio puts all of it in one workspace that opens straight from disk: images, music, a writing assistant and a new-project wizard.

🛠️ **Also on the road:**
- TLS 1.3 handshakes with Chrome, and TCP over a real Intel network card
- 256-bit vector math proven on real silicon
- A Commodore 64 booting in your browser
- 34 classic games playable in the browser, and Magic: The Gathering with all 404 cards in its card pool modelled
- ModBuilder, for building Valheim mods right in the browser
- Every release independently rebuilt and checked byte for byte: zero bytes differ outside the signature

The highway is long and we're still driving it, one verified mile at a time. 🚗
