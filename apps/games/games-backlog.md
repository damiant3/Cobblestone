# Games -- open capabilities

App-domain backlog. The shape and priority order for the platform live in
`docs/PM/CurrentPlan.md`; the platform-wide register was deleted 2026-07-23
and is not coming back. Anything that is this application's own behaviour
lives here.

The rules are the same ones: an entry says what is still missing and
nothing else, a closed entry is DELETED rather than annotated, and a
gap that is still real is never quietly dropped.

| # | Capability | State of the gap |
|---|---|---|
| GAME-10 | **There is no Chess** | The arcade has 34 games and chess is not one of them, which is the most conspicuous absence in a catalogue that already carries Go, Checkers, Othello and Backgammon. Listed in `games.json` and on the landing page as of 2026-08-26 and **not built**, so `apps/games/server.ps1:386` serves `rungame.html` for it rather than a board; that is deliberate and Damian's call, and it is what the landing page's "partially built" wording covers. What closing it needs, in the order the work falls: a board representation and move generation for all six pieces, then the three rules that are always the ones left out (castling with its four preconditions, en passant with its one-ply window, and promotion), then check, checkmate and stalemate detection, then a draw by the fifty-move rule and threefold repetition, then an AI. Note before starting that the existing board games here are the model for the AI shape: `Checkers` is minimax and `Connect Four` is alpha-beta, so the search is not new ground, but the move generator and the legality filter are where chess is genuinely harder than anything already in this directory. Nothing in the tree defines a chess move today; this is a build, not a repair. |
| GAME-9 | **`Simulate` calls two fixes that were never written** | `SimBaseline` and `SimRunner` are RED, 2 CDX3002 errors each, measured 2026-08-08 by `build/sweep-app-classes.ps1 -Jobs 8`. Both fail at the same two lines of `apps/games/codexmagic/Simulate.codex:97-98`, so it is one defect surfacing twice: `apply-screw-fix gs3 rules player` and `apply-flood-fix gs4 rules player` are called and **defined nowhere in the tree** (checked outside `build-output`). The surrounding code is complete around them -- `new-screw-a`/`new-screw-b`/`new-flood-a`/`new-flood-b` are computed just above and threaded into `make-metrics`, and `gs4`/`gs5` are consumed below -- so this reads as two functions that were planned and never landed rather than two that were deleted. Closing it means deciding what a mana-screw and a mana-flood mitigation actually do to a `GameState` under `rules`, which is a simulation-design question, not a compile fix. Nothing else in `codexmagic` calls either name. |
| GAME-7 | **The HexWar AI knows nothing about supply** | Being cut off costs a unit a point of strength each turn since 2026-08-07, and it decides games: 7 of the 13 scenarios changed outcome and two changed winner. But `ai-advance-units` does not consult `supply-map`, so a unit walks out of supply as readily as into it and never falls back to reopen a line. Cutting the enemy off is likewise not a goal the AI can pursue. The map is already computed once per side per turn and is a plain `List Integer` indexed by `hex-index`, so a scoring term costs a lookup. |
| GAME-3 | **Spider needs backtracking, and greedy is now measured rather than assumed** | The solver plays the full legal move set, orders candidates by score, and halts on its own from every seed measured (63 to 495 moves, never the cap) because a position visited set forbids re-entry. It still **completes 0 of 8 suits, and the longest same-suit run it ever builds is 4 of the 13 a suit needs** (measured 2026-08-07 across seeds 42, 7 and 88, stock exhausted, no fresh move available). Two different scoring functions reached byte-identical final tableaux, so the ceiling is the greedy commit and not the weights. `sp-check-complete` was tested directly against a hand-built K-down-to-A column and removes it correctly, so the detector is not the gap. What is left is real search: depth-first with backtracking over the move tree. Note before starting that the heap has no collector and each explored node copies ten columns. |
| GAME-8 | **Monopoly can be won more often than by luck of the deal** | Bankruptcy is reachable since 2026-08-07 (a whole colour group now charges base-rent * 25, the real board's hotel ratio, against the 2x that let every player gain about 3500 over 601 turns), but it fires on roughly one seed in four. The rest run to the 601-turn cap and are decided on cash. The reason is that a group is never assembled deliberately: the AI buys whatever it lands on and there is no trading, so a monopoly forms only when the dice hand one out. Trading is the missing mechanic. |
| GAME-6 | **Magic RNG comes from one place** | **The shuffle half is already CLOSED and this row must not be read as blocked on it.** `CardDeck.codex`'s `fy-next-random` advances through `mix-bits` and `codex/test/carddeck-shuffle` is the distribution check; FGAME-1 in `codex/foreword/game/game-backlog.md` records the closure and its re-measurement. Card order therefore ALREADY moved, so there is no pending change to sequence this work behind. What is still open is only the duplication in this app: private `rng-new` / `rng-next` / `rng-value` / `rng-mod` copies between `CodexMagic--Token` and `CodexMagic--GameRules` (measured 2026-08-07, the last duplicate-name pairs left here). `Foreword chapter Random` supplies `mix-bits` and `rand-in-range` (CL 10493); both sets of private generators should call it. Expect app-level expectations that pin card order to need re-recording when they do. |
