Codex Graphics

Five demos whose pixels are computed by Codex, running in this tab.

None of the drawing here is hand-written for the browser. Each demo is Codex source compiled by the project's own backends: the GPU work goes through the wgsl plug to WebGPU compute shaders, and the CPU work through the wasm plug to WebAssembly. The page around each one loads a module and blits what it produced.

The fireworks

Forty thousand sparks over a city skyline, and the thing computing every one of them is a Codex chapter. Shells climb from the rooftops trailing embers, burst as peonies, rings, palms and crackling shells, and light the buildings and the water as they go. The kernel is the physics: one GPU thread per spark, integrating gravity, air drag, a wind field and a turbulence term, then cooling each ember from white hot through its own colour to the red it goes out at. Space throws a shell, F calls the finale.

The GPU showcase

Forty WebGPU demos: fireworks, particle swarms, ray marching, shadow maps, ambient occlusion, physically based lighting with image-based probes. Every compute shader was generated from Codex rather than typed out, and each card has a Source button that shows you the Codex it came from.

The Earth, and a black hole

Two scenes on one page. The Earth is ray-traced from real satellite imagery, with atmospheric scattering at the rim and a day-night terminator that tracks the sun: one GPU thread per pixel intersects the sphere, finds the latitude and longitude it hit, and shades it. Press Black hole and the same page marches light through curved space instead, so the disk behind it is bent up over the shadow and under it at once, and the bright ring is light that went most of the way around. That raymarcher is a self-recursive Codex function the plug lowers to a WGSL loop. Drag to rotate either one, wheel to zoom.

The aquarium

Fifty-odd fish schooling, avoiding each other and drifting with the current, every position and every decision computed in Codex. The module keeps the whole tank in WebAssembly linear memory and the page reads the state back out each frame to draw it.

The safari

Steve Howell's original classic-feeling screensaver, ported to Codex: a drive through the savanna. The road, the animals and every polygon of every frame are decided by the module running in your browser; the page only paints the draw commands it is handed. His own grading suite runs the port against the original game frame by frame.
